# Chapter 9: The Priority — Focus, Finish Lines, and Fun

## Core Idea
Too many priorities means no priorities. Pick one measurable #1 priority (the Critical Number) per quarter/year, wrap a memorable Quarterly Theme around it, set a finish line with tiered rewards, and celebrate. This chapter also opens the Execution section (10 Rockefeller Habits in three disciplines: Priorities, Data, Meeting Rhythm) and covers Habits #1 and #2, with recaps of #4, #7, #8.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Rockefeller Habits Checklist™**: 10 execution routines that create "relentless repeatability."
  - When to use: Review every quarter (Hardy at Coastal.com) to expose blind spots; not-checked boxes mean money and time left on the table, not a dead company.
  - How: Check each routine that exists (imperfectly counts). Habit #1 (healthy team) must come first; the other nine can go in any order. Pick 1-2 per quarter; all 10 take 24-36 months.
- **Rockefeller Habit #1 — Healthy, aligned executive team**: Prerequisite to everything else.
  - When to use: Before any other execution work if trust, conflict, commitment, accountability or results problems exist (Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions).
  - How: Run Lencioni's assessment/Team Kit; have all leaders reread the book yearly; meet weekly for strategy, do monthly executive education; build trust via style assessments, shared meals/offsites, shared learning.
- **Rockefeller Habit #2 — The #1 Priority / Critical Number**: The one measurable thing that moves the company this quarter (or year), supported by 3-5 ranked Rocks.
  - When to use: Every quarter and annual plan; reserve the term "Critical Number" for the single #1 even if other metrics are nearly as important.
  - How: (1) Line up candidate initiatives like dominoes and find the lead domino, or find the constraint/bottleneck (Goldratt). (2) Benchmark the number against an external standard. (3) Check it against customer and employee input (SWOT, management survey, employee survey) before locking it. (4) Set three tiers: Red (minimum), Green (goal), Super Green (stretch). (5) Add 3-5 Rocks (OPSP column 5). (6) Pick a theme and celebration. (7) Post the number everywhere and report progress weekly.
  - Why it works / failure mode: A single target creates collective effort and knocks over other dominoes (ProService Hawaii's referral goal became the growth driver and a culture). Fails when set in isolation by "tablets from the mountaintop" with no customer/employee input or external benchmark.
- **Quarterly Theme**: Fun motif and internal marketing campaign that brings the Critical Number to life.
  - When to use: Every quarter, especially for teams new to the method (quarterly before annual).
  - How: Ask "What single thing in the next 90 days must everyone align on?" Assign a non-executive Champion/team to design the theme, posters, launch event; present it to all staff (~1 hour); show daily progress on a visible board; devote about 30 minutes of the weekly meeting to it; skip it in daily huddles; end with a celebration.
- **Reward/miss decision**: Three options when the target is missed: repeat the Critical Number, move on if momentum is sufficient, or root-cause and fix one cause next quarter.
- **Powerful Celebration Question**: At the celebration, ask "How did you do it?" and let a contributor tell the story, instead of leadership speeches.
- **Habit recaps (#4, #7, #8)**: #4 one accountable person per facet (FACe, PACe, financial line items, Advisory Board for each Key Thrust); #7 Core Values/Purpose alive in hiring, praise, reprimands; #8 employees articulate BHAG, core customer in 25 words, 3 Brand Promises, elevator pitch.

## Key Concepts
- **Critical Number**: The one measurable #1 priority for the quarter or year.
- **Lead domino / constraint**: The initiative or bottleneck whose resolution makes everything else easier.
- **Rocks**: 3-5 quarterly priorities supporting the Critical Number (OPSP column 5).
- **Super Green / Green / Red**: Stretch, target, and minimum tiers of a goal, each with its own reward.
- **Main thing sequence**: Core Purpose, BHAG, Profit per X, Brand Promise, Critical Number: one "#1" at each horizon.
- **WAG**: The "wild-ankle guess" between BHAG (Everest) and the 90-day/1-year measurable step.
- **Champion**: Employee (not executive) who creates the theme and its collateral.
- **People vs. Process balance**: Alternate themes between people-side and process-side goals.

## Mental Models
- Use the **domino/constraint test** when many initiatives compete: pick the one that unlocks the others.
- Use **tiered targets** when a team is new to goals: three medals raise the odds of a win; keep the goal fixed mid-quarter and keep rewards mostly fun.
- Use **short quarterly horizons** when the culture has taken hits: quick wins rebuild momentum.
- Use **sequenced #1s** instead of parallel ones: one priority at each time scale (day, week, quarter, year, decade).

## Anti-patterns
- **Setting the Critical Number in isolation**: skips customer/employee input and external benchmarks, so staff see it as invented.
- **Moving the goal mid-quarter**: kills the chance of a late push and teaches that targets are negotiable.
- **Tying paychecks to an unproven aggressive goal**: punishes people for leadership's miscalibration; start with fun rewards, raise stakes as maturity grows (SRC ties about 15% of pay).
- **Executives building the theme themselves**: adds work to the senior team and reduces engagement.
- **Skipping the celebration after a miss**: still convene the team and announce results (the "soup kitchen" barbecue).
- **Starting with annual themes**: it takes several quarters to master choosing Critical Numbers.

## Worked Example
The City Bin Co. (Galway, Ireland; $25M, 120 employees) runs one theme per quarter. "Saving Mrs. Ryan": win 10,000 homemaker customers in 90 days (Super Green 12,000) with movie-poster visuals, a daily contract board, weekly Rock slides, monthly bonuses. "Life Begins at 40": raise earnings 40,000 euros/month during a recession (achieved). "180 to One": 60 staff job-share 3 days each to build empathy (celebrated at the dog races). "Bin it": staff submit wasteful tasks, 150+ activities killed. "Bin's Health": cost cuts, goal missed, party held but no awards. Browne alternates people-side and process-side themes.

## Key Takeaways
1. One measurable priority per period beats many; sequence #1s from the day to the decade.
2. Healthy team first (Habit #1), then the Critical Number (Habit #2).
3. Find the Critical Number via lead domino or constraint, validated by customer and employee input.
4. Use Red/Green/Super Green tiers and mostly-fun rewards; never change the goal midstream.
5. Delegate theme creation to non-executives; celebrate with "How did you do it?"
6. After a miss: repeat, move on, or root-cause, but always convene.
