# Chapter 10: The Data — Powering Prediction

## Core Idea
A leader's fundamental job is prediction (Deming), which needs both quantitative data and human intelligence-gathering. Talk weekly with employees and customers, share what you learn at the weekly executive meeting, give everyone KPIs, and make scores visible everywhere, with middle managers carrying the load.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Learn fast; act fast cycle (Wal-Mart)**: Gather qualitative intelligence in the market, combine with quantitative data, decide, and implement corrections quickly.
  - When to use: From day one and permanently; speed (even 10 days ahead) is the advantage.
  - How: Leaders are in the market about 80% of the week; review data plus field intelligence at one weekly session; implement corrections immediately. GE's Quick Market Intelligence: salespeople report competitor and customer news daily or weekly.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Competitors who read last week's numbers on Monday are 10 days behind. Leaders stop the habit because feedback sounds repetitive, but one or two ideas can fuel a business model.
- **Rockefeller Habit #5 — Employee input (Start/Stop/Keep)**: Every executive and middle manager has a 15-45 minute Start/Stop/Keep conversation with at least one employee weekly.
  - When to use: Weekly; prioritize customer-facing staff and newest hires (fresh eyes).
  - How: Ask what we should start, stop, keep doing; ask once more at the end because people open up late; stress the "stop" answers (they destroy motivation); report insights in a 10-minute slot of the weekly executive meeting; send actionable ideas to a formal suggestion process. Prompt submissions with three buckets: increase revenue, reduce costs, make things easier/better. Optional: 3I program (each manager submits 3 ideas monthly; CEO responds and awards top 20), 3M 15% time, ShipIt days.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Closes the leak of ideas walking out the door. Backfires if the loop isn't closed, so a middle-management team owns responses, tracks a **Suggestion Aging Report** (ideas 30/60/90 days old), and posts suggestions transparently (never negative items about individuals).
- **Rockefeller Habit #6 — Customer input (4Q conversation)**: Every executive and middle manager has a 4Q conversation with at least one end user weekly, in person, not by survey.
  - When to use: Weekly; in B2B bypass channel/purchasing with permission and match counterparts (CFO to CFO).
  - How: Ask (1) How are you doing? (2) What's going on in your industry/neighborhood? (3) What do you hear about our competitors? (4) How are we doing? Ask Q4 last; the call is about them. Salespeople ask what the contact's bonus is tied to. Share at the weekly meeting; middle management closes the loop under the customer advocacy owner.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Question 3 cuts through internal bias. At Coastal.com it showed customers valued speed, not low prices; overnight shipping lifted one market's sales 60%.
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**: Net percentage of customers actively recommending you; measures advocacy, not passive satisfaction. Use with a follow-up call (Gung Ho! Pizza links NPS to bonuses).
- **Rockefeller Habit #9 — KPIs for everyone**: Each person reports 1-2 weekly KPIs, owns 1 Critical Number aligned with the company's, and 3-5 Rocks; executives and managers have a coach or peer coach.
  - When to use: After the OPSP exists; cascades column 7 to every role.
  - How: Define KPIs so each person can answer "Did I have a great day/week?"; display on whiteboard, printed charts, or dashboards; pick five behaviors to start/stop and report daily to a peer coach (Goldsmith method).
- **Rockefeller Habit #10 — Scoreboards everywhere**: Plans and performance visible to all.
  - How: Create a "situation room" (physical or a dedicated conference line) for weekly meetings with OPSP, KPIs, and priorities posted; post Core Values/Purpose; replicate at store level; above about 50 employees or multiple sites, adopt accountability software (e.g., Align) to track cascading priorities.
- **Salesperson daily call-in**: Daily 3-minute voicemail of good news, customer/competitor feedback and barriers (transcribed to text); never ask for written reports.

## Key Concepts
- **Prediction**: Leadership's core task, enabled by data plus gut feel from the market.
- **Close the loop**: Respond to every suggestion, even to say why not.
- **Stop doing**: Employee-reported practices that drain motivation.
- **Line of sight**: Each employee's Critical Number and Rocks tie to the company's.
- **Situation room**: Visible home for metrics, goals, and plans used in weekly meetings.
- **Idea Flash**: Appletree's CRM app for frontline idea capture (8,800 ideas in 90 days).
- **Triple scorecard**: QuEST's customer, employee, and company satisfaction (q-stat gates project acceptance).
- **Red/yellow/green**: Mulally's color-coded charts ("you can't manage a secret").

## Mental Models
- Use **big data + human intelligence** together when deciding: numbers show what, conversations show why.
- Use **the middle-management team as the loop-closer** when the senior team is overloaded.
- Use **"great vs. good" meeting test**: great companies spend roughly 20% of the weekly executive meeting on customer feedback (Bain/Reichheld).
- Use **the scoreboard standard** when unsure what to post: anyone should see the score at a glance, like a stadium.

## Anti-patterns
- **Gathering feedback with no follow-through**: employees conclude it's pointless.
- **Written reports for field intelligence**: they don't get written; use voice or short verbal sharing.
- **Dropping calls when feedback sounds repetitive**: you lose the one idea that matters.
- **Customer neglect while chasing new logos**: lost customers could fuel at least half of growth.
- **Metrics only (or gut only)**: each is an incomplete view.

## Worked Example
Coastal.com (Roger Hardy): after a two-day planning session lacking growth ideas, leaders called 30-40 customers every Friday. The same theme emerged: customers wanted contact lenses next day. They overnighted everything, and one market's sales rose 60%. Question 3 also showed customers raved about service, so they did not cut prices against low-price rivals. When calls stopped yielding responses, they moved to post-purchase NPS surveys via SurveyMonkey and Facebook comments. Company later sold for CAD$430M.

## Key Takeaways
1. Spend about 80% of the week in the market; learn fast, act fast.
2. Weekly Start/Stop/Keep with employees (Habit #5) and 4Q with customers (Habit #6).
3. Share insights verbally at the weekly meeting; close the loop through middle management.
4. Every person has 1-2 weekly KPIs and one aligned Critical Number (Habit #9).
5. Make scores visible: situation room, wall posters, dashboards (Habit #10).
6. Track NPS and act on it.
