# Chapter 5: The Managers (Coaches) — Keeping and Growing (Educating) the Team

## Core Idea
People join companies and leave managers, so keep talent engaged by building middle managers who act as coaches, and grow people through training, onboarding, and regular one-on-one coaching. Past ~100 employees, the lack of capable managers is a main growth barrier.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five management practices (in reverse order of importance)**: coached in one-on-ones with "direct supports": 5. Hire fewer people, pay them more; 4. Give recognition and show appreciation; 3. Set clear expectations and a line of sight; 2. Don't demotivate, "dehassle"; 1. Help people play to their strengths.
  - When to use: as the agenda for every manager's coaching and for diagnosing disengagement.
  - How: see practices below.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Gallup says managers drive at least 70% of variance in engagement; Google's analytics found one-on-one coaching the top leader trait. Failure mode: using perks (free lunch, yoga) in place of good managers.
- **Hire less, pay more**: select rigorously (Topgrading), pay above market, train heavily, so total wage cost as % of revenue falls.
  - When to use: frontline and professional roles where productivity scales with talent.
  - How: pay above industry; fit pay structure (commission vs. flat hourly) to Core Values and Brand Promise; customize packages for key people ("fair" is not "same").
  - Evidence: Container Store "1 Equals 3" (hires ~3%, pays 50-100% more, 263 training hours); Costco ~70% higher pay, ~40% fewer staff per revenue, 6% vs. 21% turnover; Goldman Sachs.
- **Recognition (3:1 ratio)**: at least three positive interactions per negative one.
  - How: thank daily; test public vs. private, material vs. immaterial, peer vs. boss until you find each person's trigger.
- **Line of sight**: employees can explain how their work delivers Purpose, strategy, and Brand Promise; define the what, not the how.
  - How: use OPSP column 7 per quarter: 2-3 KPIs, a handful of Priorities, and a Critical Number (main bottleneck to fix); sit down immediately if KPIs are missed.
- **Dehassling**: remove demotivators, mainly people and processes.
  - How: remove B/C Players who slow A Players, fire abusive clients, fix tools and lame policies, use Lean.
- **Strengths-based management (love/loathe exercise)**: a strength gives you energy; a weakness drains you even if you're good at it.
  - When to use: when a department asks for more headcount, before hiring.
  - How: (1) Staff take Gallup StrengthsFinder. (2) Document activities loved and loathed for 2 weeks. (3) Eliminate what no one should do. (4) Turn the remainder into a Job Scorecard for a new chess piece who loves it. (5) Apply to yourself.
  - Why it works / failure mode: energy drives performance and loyalty. Failure mode: managers describe teams in generalities and add more of the same people.
- **Onboarding as imprinting**: celebrate on day one and run a formal orientation built around real work and Core Values with senior leaders involved.
- **Training investment benchmarks**: 2-3% of payroll; suggested annual hours 12 frontline, 25 middle managers, 45-60 senior leaders; prioritize middle managers first.
- **Situational Leadership (Blanchard/Hersey)**: vary direction and support by the person's competence and confidence per task until fully delegated.
- **Regular one-on-one coaching conversation**: replaces annual reviews. Weekly 20-30 min or monthly one hour, same day, rarely cancelled.
  - How: review KPIs, Priorities, Critical Number; recognize, analyze gaps, give timely feedback on Core Values; vary tasks to stretch; frame development as rock-climbing, not a ladder.

## Key Concepts
- **Direct supports**: replace "direct reports" to signal servant leadership (Fathom).
- **Manager vs. leader**: managing is about differences between people; leading is about universals and a shared vision (Buckingham).
- **Coach**: preferred term over "manager" (e.g., sales coach).
- **Critical Number**: the main bottleneck a person or team must fix this quarter.
- **Garbage University**: City Bin's internal academy, 3 hours every two weeks Sept-May, with annual theme and syllabus.
- **Culture leak**: around 70 employees culture starts to weaken without formal orientation (Sapient).
- **Imprinting**: new hires are most receptive in early weeks, like hatchlings.
- **Reflective dialogue**: MOM's 30-60 min discussion after a group-watched webcast, ending with "what is the one takeaway you will commit to?".
- **Human Resources renaming**: avoid "resources"; use Talent Development, People Support, etc.

## Mental Models
- Use "dehassle before motivate" when engagement is slipping: find the people and process blockers first.
- Use "love/loathe before hire" when a team asks for headcount: reshape existing jobs first.
- Use "what, not how" when delegating: set outcomes and KPIs, leave method to the person.
- Use "rock-climbing, not ladder" when someone wants growth in a flat organization: offer lateral challenges aligned with strengths.

## Anti-patterns
- **Perks instead of great managers**: engagement follows the manager.
- **Copying a competitor's compensation or buying a survey to match the market**: leads to average.
- **Same recognition for everyone**: triggers differ.
- **Onboarding by paperwork or a 200-page binder, or no desk on day one**: wastes imprinting.
- **Annual or quarterly reviews only**: too far from behavior.
- **Cancelling one-on-ones**: signals they don't matter.
- **Cutting training because people might leave**: research shows training raises loyalty.
- **Relying on a charismatic leader past ~50-100 employees**: need managers who practice the five habits.

## Worked Example
Sapient (Boston) at about 70 employees: a former second-grade teacher on staff warned that culture was "leaking." She designed a five-day boot camp where new hires worked on nagging internal projects while one Core Value was reinforced each day. Result: recruits were near 100% field-ready after a week instead of a six-month ramp-up, and the approach held as the firm scaled to 2,500 people. Aquire (Lois Melbourne): instead of hiring scarce programmers, programmers logged energy-draining activities; useless tasks were dropped, and the rest became a Job Scorecard for a new hire who loves them, making existing programmers happier and more productive.

## Key Takeaways
1. People leave managers; invest in middle managers as coaches, especially past 100 employees.
2. Run the five practices in order of importance: strengths, dehassle, line of sight, recognition (3:1), fewer people paid more.
3. Use love/loathe and StrengthsFinder to refocus jobs before adding headcount.
4. Treat onboarding as a celebration and a values-centered orientation with senior leaders.
5. Budget 2-3% of payroll for training; focus first on middle managers; set hours per level.
6. Hold regular one-on-ones using KPIs, Priorities, and Critical Number, with Situational Leadership.
7. Rename "direct reports" to "direct supports" and HR to something people-centered.
