# Chapter 7: The 7 Strata of Strategy — The Framework for Dominating Your Industry

## Core Idea
The 7 Strata of Strategy is a one-page worksheet that integrates Porter, Collins, Frei, Bloom, and others into a coherent, differentiated strategy for mid-market firms: the "page behind the page" of the OPSP. Work it weekly with a small strategic thinking team; each stratum reinforces the others.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 7 Strata of Strategy**: (1) Words You Own (Mindshare); (2) Sandbox and Brand Promises; (3) Brand Promise Guarantee (Catalytic Mechanism); (4) One-PHRASE Strategy (Key to Making Money); (5) Differentiating Activities (3 to 5 Hows); (6) X-Factor (10x-100x Underlying Advantage); (7) Profit per X (Economic Engine) and BHAG (10- to 25-year goal).
  - When to use: When growth is stalling, margins are commoditizing, or you can't state your strategy simply. Skip it if you are already growing fast with fat margins.
  - How: Name a 3-5 person strategic thinking team; meet about an hour weekly; assign each member one Key Resource to master and brief; fill the worksheet, also noting how competitors and admired outside firms would fill each stratum; summarize some answers into OPSP columns 2-3; keep the One-PHRASE and X-Factor confidential; "nail down what you can and keep moving."
  - Why it works / failure mode: Each layer constrains the next (words, promises, guarantee, trade-offs, activities, hidden advantage, economics), so competitors can copy pieces but not the whole. Failure: treating it as a sequential engineering exercise or one-off offsite; it is messy, iterative, and can take years (AutoNation).
- **Stratum 1, Words You Own**: Branding = owning a small piece of mind-space (Volvo: safety; BMW: driving experience; Glacéau: "enhanced waters").
  - When to use: First, because it drives revenue most. 
  - How: Pick words customers actually search; test rank in Google/Bing; check volume and competition in the Keyword Planner; prefer slightly less popular but specific terms; publish unbiased educational content ("You are what you publish"). To hurt a rival, steal its word (Google vs. Yahoo: "search").
- **Stratum 2, Sandbox and Brand Promises** (four decisions): Who/Where (the "juicy red" core customer, defined beyond demographics, found in a profit pool of ~10% of customers with outsized profit); What (a 100% solution sold on emotion, justified by logic); three measurable Brand Promises (one leads); Kept Promise Indicators (KPIs) measured daily.
  - When to use: To define the market you will serve and your reasons to buy.
  - How: Avoid vague "quality," "value," "service"; quantify (BuildDirect: 40-50% lower price); assign a team to monitor daily; exit products where you can't keep the promise.
- **Brand Promise Guarantee (Catalytic Mechanism)**: Make breaking a promise hurt so the team stays focused (RedBalloon 100% refund; Oracle $1M/$10M performance guarantees; "short pay" for services; BuildDirect 30-day free returns). It also reduces buyer fear.
- **One-PHRASE Strategy**: The key business-model lever that drives profit and decides which customer wants to ignore (IKEA: flat pack; Apple: closed architecture; Southwest: "Wheels Up").
  - When to use: When promises and guarantees are eroding margins under "want, want, wanting" demands.
  - How: Be the best at a few promises for a fanatical ~7% and dare to be the worst at everything else; choose trade-offs rivals can't stomach.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Trade-offs create blocking strategies. Failure: pleasing everyone yields "average." Keep it secret early.
- **Differentiating Activities (3 to 5 Hows)**: Differentiation lives at the activity level (Porter); it is something competitors won't or can't do without great effort. Combination matters (BuildDirect: pallet minimum, no name brands, cash in advance; Southwest: one aircraft type, second-tier airports, point-to-point, no reserved seats).
- **X-Factor (10x-100x)**: A hidden underlying advantage attacking an industry choke point.
  - How: Ask "What do I hate most about my industry? What is the choke point (cost or time)?" Review titles of your last 10 trade-association breakout sessions for patterns; then brainstorm a 10x fix.
- **Profit per X and BHAG**: One economic-engine KPI (fixed denominator tied to the One-PHRASE) plus a 10-25 year goal measured in the same X.
  - How: Choose X different from industry norm (Southwest: plane; RedBalloon: experience; BuildDirect: building product category); set BHAG in units of X and aligned with Purpose.
- **Strategic Thinking Team ("the Council")**: Advisory (not consensus) group per Collins's *Good to Great*; Hamel's "headlight team"; members talk weekly to customers and competitors. Marketing strategy = strategy; 4Ps (or Ogilvy's 4Es) are the companion agenda.

## Key Concepts
- **Sandbox**: What you sell to whom and where.
- **Juicy red core customer**: The customer segment yielding most profit over time.
- **Profit pool**: A small niche (<=10% of customers) holding disproportionate profit.
- **Brand Promises**: Three measurable reasons to buy, one leading.
- **Kept Promise Indicators**: Daily metrics showing promises are being kept.
- **Catalytic mechanism**: A guarantee that makes breaking a promise painful.
- **Blocking strategy**: A trade-off competitors won't match.
- **Choke point**: Industry-wide constraint accepted as "the way it is."
- **Profit per X**: The single KPI of your unique scaling logic.
- **BHAG**: Measurable 10-25 year goal aligned to Purpose and Profit per X.

## Mental Models
- **Dare to be bad**: Use when promises keep expanding; pick what to be worst at.
- **Page behind the page**: Use when OPSP answers feel shallow; return to the worksheet.
- **Headlights, not consensus**: Use when running the council; advisors illuminate, the CEO decides.
- **Steal their word**: Use when attacking an incumbent's mindshare.

## Anti-patterns
- **Vague promises ("quality," "value," "service")**: Mean different things to different customers; can't be measured.
- **Chasing every customer desire**: The feature war erodes margins.
- **Defining customers by demographics only**: Misses the persona and where to reach her.
- **Annual-only strategy sessions**: Strategy needs weekly iteration.
- **Revealing the One-PHRASE or X-Factor**: Hands competitors the engine.
- **Shiny-object syndrome**: Leaving the 100% solution unfinished.

## Worked Example
BuildDirect.com (building materials online, doubled sales yearly since 2003, ~$200M run rate in 2014). Words: "laminate flooring," "hardwood flooring," built via an unbiased Learning Center. Sandbox: "Debby the Do-It-Yourselfer." Promises: best price (40-50% lower, lead), best customer service, product expertise, tracked by daily price monitoring and response times. Guarantee: 30-day no-questions returns with free return shipping. One-PHRASE: secret. Activities: pallet minimum, no name brands, cash in advance. X-Factor: secret, a ~10x edge. Profit per building product category; BHAG: dominate 20 of 1,000+ categories (about $500M-$2B each) for $10-20B revenue.

## Key Takeaways
1. Own a word customers search for; it is the biggest revenue lever.
2. State three measurable Brand Promises and track them daily.
3. Back promises with a guarantee that hurts to break.
4. Profit comes from a One-PHRASE lever and deliberate, uncomfortable trade-offs.
5. Differentiation is a hard-to-copy combination of activities; defend it with a 10x X-Factor.
6. Set Profit per X and a BHAG in the same unit.
7. Keep a small council meeting weekly; trust the process.
