# Chapter 6: The Core — Values, Purpose, and Competencies

## Core Idea
Past 50–70 employees, "culture drift" sets in, so the firm must discover, codify, and relentlessly reinforce its Core (Values, Purpose, Competencies). The Core links People to Strategy in the 4 Decisions model and anchors the left side of the OPSP. Strategy intro: a strategy is working when gross margin dollars and revenue grow, and you say "no" ~20x more than "yes."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Core Values**: Rules and boundaries that define the company's personality and give a final "Should/Shouldn't" test for every behavior and decision.
  - When to use: Whenever you need people to decide without asking permission ("consult the Core Values"), hire, review performance, or vet customers and acquisitions.
  - How: (1) Treat it as DISCOVERY, not a wish list: mine "artifacts" (stories, behaviors) like an archaeologist. (2) Write each Value as a phrase (3–8 total), not a single word. (3) Let the list "bake" a year; at each quarterly planning ask "Are there plenty of examples where we lived this?" Yes = Core Value; no = wish-list item or weakened value. (4) Describe each with a phrase, a sentence or two, and a real story/legend (Tyabji's "blue book").
  - Why it works / failure mode: Values are neither good nor bad, they just are; accuracy beats aspiration. Failure: generic platitudes ("integrity, teamwork"), carving the list in stone on day one, or changing Values to fit an acquisition (Sapient's biggest mistake; it recovered only by reverting).
- **Core Purpose**: The "Why" (heart of the firm), usually built around one word or idea (3M: innovation; Disney: happiness).
  - When to use: To ignite discretionary effort (research cited: ~40% more) and to drive a CEO "stump speech."
  - How: Gather a team; ask "What do we do?"; ask "Why?" five times until you hit "save the world," then back up one step. Expand the single word into a phrase or two.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Failure is describing Brand Promises (e.g., "best talent, save time") instead of Purpose ("Helping people succeed"). Money-making is not a Purpose.
- **Core Competencies** (Prahalad and Hamel): Inherent strengths that (1) competitors can't easily imitate, (2) can be reused across many products/markets, (3) contribute to customer-perceived benefit.
  - When to use: To set boundaries on which offerings to pursue and to find differentiation. Also name core weaknesses (3M can't sell direct, so it excels with distributors).
  - How: Strategic thinking team reads the HBR article, then debates; define broadly (BIC = "disposable plastic anything," not "cheap pens").
- **8 Ways to Bring the Core Alive** (align ALL HR systems around one list): Storytelling, Recruitment and Selection, Onboarding, Performance Appraisals and Handbooks, Recognition and Rewards, Newsletters, Themes, Everyday Reinforcement (the most important).
  - When to use: As the audit list for any HR hodgepodge of random criteria lists, reward lists, and generic handbooks.
  - How: Collect legends per Value; score candidates per Value; teach Values on day one; hang appraisals and handbook sections on Values; reward per Value; feature one Value per newsletter; make one Value the quarterly theme; tie every praise and reprimand to a Value or the Purpose.
- **Mission / Vision / Values terminology**: "Purpose" replaces "mission"; "Vision" = the whole OPSP (not a forgettable paragraph); keep the elements separate so people remember them. Pick terms and use them consistently.

## Key Concepts
- **Culture drift**: Loss of cultural cohesion once leaders no longer know everyone's name (~50–70 people).
- **Core Values**: A handful of phrase-form rules that define company personality; discovered, not invented.
- **Core Purpose**: The deeper "Why," centered on one word or idea.
- **Core Competency**: A hard-to-copy, reusable strength that customers feel.
- **Core weakness**: What the firm is inherently bad at; defines what to avoid or partner around.
- **Stump speech**: Repeated CEO talk built from the Purpose to remind everyone of the big picture.
- **Core Values Hall of Fame**: Quarterly event where employees submit stories of colleagues living a Value.
- **Employee Experience Department**: Appletree's rename of HR to focus on experience.
- **"Merger" is a myth**: Only acquisitions exist; acquired staff need fast induction into the acquirer's culture.

## Mental Models
- **Strong core muscles**: Use when diagnosing instability or lost focus during scaling; Values, Purpose, Competencies are the three stabilizers.
- **Parenting**: Use when tempted to over-explain; keep a handful of rules, repeat yourself for years (Tyabji: "spent 11 years repeating myself").
- **Archaeology, not wish list**: Use when defining Values; look for artifacts that already exist.
- **Say yes until you can afford no**: Use when judging strategy maturity; a real strategy produces 20 "nos" per "yes."

## Anti-patterns
- **Posting Values on the wall / laminated cards**: Without aligning hiring, appraisal, rewards, and daily decisions, they stay decoration.
- **Wish-list or platitude Values**: They miss the real culture and confuse the organization.
- **Changing Values to fit an acquisition or fad**: Triggers "endless therapy"; the Core is a personality, not a policy.
- **Purpose that is a Brand Promise or "make money"**: Doesn't capture hearts.
- **Defining Competencies too narrowly**: Closes growth paths (BIC).
- **Treating Values as a marketing initiative**: They must drive internal decisions, including firing customers.

## Worked Example
Appletree Answers (call centers, 24 acquisitions, 650 employees). Problem: frontline turnover 110% (industry ~200%) with 13 acquisitions fragmenting culture. Values (7): Integrity matters; Think like a customer; Spirited fun; Be quick, but don't hurry; Employees are critical; Small details are huge; Take care of each other. Purpose: "Enhancing the lives of customers and employees, one interaction at a time" (25M calls/year). Moves: "Dream On" employee wish program (from the Value "Take care of each other") exposed real frontline hardship (e.g., 17 wished for a car) and cut turnover to ~20% with ~20x ROI; quarterly Hall of Fame with a winner per Value; Value-based hiring (candidates "light up" or not); day-one acquisition onboarding by staff from other sites; appraisals scored on Values ("How does that marry with 'Be quick, but don't hurry'?"); fired abusive customers, declined low-integrity clients. Results: turnover under 20%, profit 21% vs 4% industry, a 14x sale multiple vs ~3x, and a 10-point EBITDA lift at newly acquired sites.

## Key Takeaways
1. Discover Values from real stories; validate them over a year of quarterly reviews before locking.
2. Purpose is one word or idea reached via five whys; don't confuse it with Brand Promises.
3. Define Competencies broadly and name core weaknesses to set strategic boundaries.
4. Align every HR system (hire, onboard, appraise, reward, communicate) to one list of Values and Purpose.
5. Daily manager reinforcement, tying every praise or reprimand to a Value, matters most.
6. Acquisitions require a day-one culture-induction process; never change Values to accommodate a deal.
7. "Soft" Core work pays off in measurable turnover, margin, and valuation.
