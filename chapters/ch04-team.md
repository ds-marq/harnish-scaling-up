# Chapter 4: The Team — Attracting and Hiring

## Core Idea
Hiring A Players at every level needs two things: marketing actively recruiting a large pool of culture-fit candidates, and Topgrading to select from it rigorously. A bad hire costs about 15x annual salary, so treat recruiting like landing the right customers.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Job Scorecard**: a replacement for the job description, built from the person's purpose, 3-10 specific measurable outcomes for the next 1-3 years, and the technical and cultural competencies required.
  - When to use: before starting any search, executive or frontline.
  - How: (1) State the purpose. (2) Write outcomes as results, not activities ($8M revenue, seven new key clients, 100% renewal rate). (3) List competencies aligned to Core Values and the Brand Promise. (4) Use outcomes to test candidates ("does their history support taking us from $20M to $35M?").
  - Why it works / failure mode: outcomes let you judge a candidate's capacity to deliver; descriptions only list tasks. Failure mode: keeping job descriptions or hiring for generic skills.
- **Topgrading**: hiring method (Brad and Geoff Smart) that gets the right hire 90%+ of the time versus 25-60% for gut, testing, or standard behavioral interviews. An A Player is top 10% of the talent pool willing to take your specific offer.
  - When to use: every hire; scale interview depth by level.
  - How: (1) Attract 20+ applicants per position. (2) Screen with gatekeeper application questions and online tests (20 to 10). (3) Screening interview with five questions, 30-45 min (10 to 3). (4) CIDS interview on the top three (3-4 hours for leaders, 1-2 hours for entry level). (5) Use TORC (Threat of Reference Check). (6) Test-drive before committing. (7) Hire the one who can deliver 150% of the Scorecard.
  - Why it works / failure mode: past performance predicts future performance; a long interview exposes polished interviewees and relaxes shy ones. Failure mode: hiring whoever "can fog the mirror" under growth stress.
- **CIDS interview (Chronological In-Depth Structured)**: walk the entire career job by job, asking about each boss, reasons for leaving, and "what will so-and-so say about you?".
  - When to use: final-three stage.
- **Recruiting as (Guerilla) Marketing**: market jobs to the specific "strange" talent pool where it already gathers.
  - When to use: when your warm network is exhausted or the role is hard to fill.
  - How: put ads where your Purpose-driven people spend time; write the ad in your culture's voice ("You: ... We: ..."); use stunts, a book, a blog, or a Best Places to Work list.
- **Will, Values, Results, Skill (WVRS)**: four hiring criteria in strict order of importance.
  - When to use: ranking finalists. Will = desire to excel, courage, learning; Values = fit with Core Values; Results = can deliver KPIs; Skills = least important (skill sets age out in about 5 years).
- **Test-driving candidates**: temp-to-perm, evening consulting, or paid-to-quit offers (Zappos $3,000; Amazon Pay to Quit $2,000 rising to $5,000).

## Key Concepts
- **A Player**: top 10% of available talent who will accept your offer; McDonald's has the same chance as Goldman Sachs.
- **Strange workforce**: from Dan Cable, differentiated strategy needs a differentiated (not normal) workforce and HR systems.
- **Chess vs. checkers**: great teams use specialists whose strengths differ, not look-alike generalists.
- **Idiot savant**: a person extremely talented at one thing, possibly weak elsewhere; the team is well-rounded, individuals need not be.
- **20:1 ratio**: minimum applicants per opening to raise the odds of hiring an A Player.
- **Culture as immune system**: it rejects capable people who don't match Core Values.
- **TORC**: Threat of Reference Check, a "truth serum" in interviews.
- **Gatekeeper questions**: application questions revealing values (e.g., "What companies do you admire?").

## Mental Models
- Use "fish where there are fish" when recruiting: go to where people who share your Purpose already hang out.
- Use "hire for fit, train for skill" when choosing between a skilled mismatch and a learnable fit.
- Use "cost of the interview vs. cost of the miss" when someone says 3-4 hours is too long: it is small versus thousands of hours of headaches.
- Use chess thinking when a team lacks capacity: add a specialist whose strengths fill the gap instead of another generalist.

## Anti-patterns
- **Job descriptions**: list activities, not outcomes.
- **Feel-good interviews**: 25-60% success; we hire people like ourselves.
- **Waiting for candidates to "fall from the sky"**: best candidates are employed elsewhere and need a reason to come.
- **Hiring on skills first**: skills are the least important and age fast.
- **Posting on the same job boards as competitors**: no differentiation.
- **Over-polished culture shortcuts**: skipping reference checks on bosses.

## Worked Example
MOM's Organic Market needed a CFO who would embrace its Purpose (protect and restore the environment). Instead of a recruiter or job boards, CEO Scott Nash ran an ad on Treehugger.com with culture-laden questions ("Are you an entrepreneur in a CPA's body? Would you rather work in jeans?"). In a week: 40 resumes from Purpose-aligned candidates. Nash led a 3-4 hour Topgrading interview (full work history, boss names, "what will they say about you?", questions about a difficult boss, teenage money habits, last passionate debate) and hired Kelly Moler, who helped grow revenue to $130M over eight years. Frontline "Join us" ads in the same voice raised retention sharply.

## Key Takeaways
1. A bad hire costs about 15x salary; invest in the process.
2. Write a Job Scorecard (outcomes plus competencies) before any search.
3. Marketing must build a pool of 20+ candidates per role, aimed at your culture's people.
4. Use Topgrading: screen, then CIDS with TORC, then test-drive.
5. Rank candidates by Will, Values, Results, Skills, in that order.
6. Hire specialists and "strange" people, not look-alikes.
7. A great reputation (Best Places to Work, CEO book, blog) multiplies applicants, as at Rackspace (52,000 for 569 openings).
